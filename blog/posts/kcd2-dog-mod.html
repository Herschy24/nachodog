Making Mutt look like Nacho in Kingdom Come: Deliverance II

Henry already has a loyal dog. I wanted it to be mine: a tall black greyhound with a frosted muzzle and white socks.

In Kingdom Come: Deliverance II, Henry's companion is Mutt, a scruffy, loyal mongrel who follows you around Bohemia. I have a loyal dog who follows me around the house: Nacho, a tall black female greyhound with a grey muzzle, a white chest and white socks. People had already made mods that swap Mutt for other breeds, so I set out, with Claude doing most of the hands-on work in Blender, to make Mutt into Nacho.

Why a recolour wasn't enough

I started from an existing mod that turns Mutt into a Dobermann. Unpacking it showed it only replaces Mutt's texture and material settings, with the same body underneath. A greyhound is a different shape altogether: deep chest, tucked waist, thin legs, long narrow head. That meant changing the 3D model itself.

Reshaping Mutt instead of replacing him

The usual way to put a new model into the game is to rig it to the game's dog skeleton and export it through CryEngine's tools, which is fiddly and easy to break. We went the other way. I found a free, rigged whippet model (CC-0, by Patrick W. Crawford), and Claude reshaped Mutt's own mesh in Blender until it matched the whippet's body.

Because the mesh is still Mutt's, it keeps his skeleton, his weights and every one of his animations. The new vertex positions were written straight back into the game's .skin file, with no exporter involved. Bending the legs, neck, jaw and tail with Mutt's rig in Blender was the check that the new body still moved properly.

From whippet to Nacho

A whippet was the right body shape but the wrong dog. The next round repainted the coat to match her: black all over, salt-and-pepper grey on the muzzle and chin, a white chest patch and white toes and socks. Mutt is male and Nacho isn't, so that got fixed in the mesh too.

Then came a lot of rounds against photos of her. The side view needed a longer muzzle, about 15% longer, with the wedge-shaped profile and blunt rounded nose greyhounds have. The front view needed a head that's widest across the skull and narrows to a fine nose, rather than being widest at the jowls. Then the chin, lips, brow and nostrils got smaller fixes. In all there were eleven preview renders before she looked right from every angle.

The bugs

Most of the time went on things that looked fine in Blender and wrong in the game.

  • She came out grey. The base black was about three times too light, and a setting copied from the Dobermann mod (ScratchLevel="0.84") lays a lighter fur layer over the whole body. Darkening the coat and setting that to 0 fixed it.
  • Then she came out green. The black was now so dark that the game's texture compression stored slightly more green than red or blue, and the game's fur tint boosted that difference into a green dog. The fix was to turn fur saturation down to 0, which suits a black, grey and white dog anyway.
  • Her face broke when she panted. The game opens Mutt's jaw wide when he pants, and that dragged bits of the reshaped snout down with it. The head was rebuilt from Mutt's original head, narrowed and lengthened, so the jaw, lips and teeth move the way the animations expect.
  • The head was too big. Greyhounds have small heads. It got shrunk around the skull: narrower, lower and slightly shorter.
  • Updates silently didn't install. For a while it looked like the game was locking the mod file. It turned out Blender was holding it open from an earlier check, so a couple of "installed" fixes never actually landed.

Her name

The last step was the name. Mutt is now Nacho in 89 English text strings: her name tag, the obedience stat, perks, quest titles and logs, item descriptions and tutorials. Henry still says "Mutt" out loud, because that's recorded voice acting, and the spoken subtitles match the voice.

What I couldn't fix

She's the right shape but a bit small. In game she stands about 66 cm at the shoulder; a tall female greyhound like Nacho is more like 71 to 74 cm. Her size comes from Mutt's skeleton and animations, not the mesh, so making her taller would mean scaling the skeleton, which risks breaking every animation and every other dog that shares it. She's believably a greyhound, just a petite one.
