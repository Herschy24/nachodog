Can AI design a stronger carabiner?

I asked AI to help design a stronger carabiner. It was a useful sketching partner and a terrible engineer.

Carabiners are a deceptively simple part: a bent loop with a gate. But the shape is the result of decades of engineering around how load actually flows through the metal. I wanted to see whether an AI could take a standard design and make it stronger, and, just as interesting, where it would fall short.

Safety first: nothing in this post is climbing gear. A home-designed or 3D-printed carabiner is not rated, not tested and must never hold a person. This was an experiment about AI, not a product.

The goal

What it got right

The good bits: e.g. fast iteration, sensible-looking geometry, thickening the spine, rounding sharp corners.

The limitations

This is the real point of the project. The places I'd expect an AI to struggle with a load-bearing part:

  • It can't verify its own claims. "This is 30% stronger" means nothing without simulation or a physical pull test.
  • Load paths and stress concentrations. Strength lives in the spine and the curves; small geometry changes can move the weak point somewhere unexpected.
  • Material assumptions. Real carabiners are forged aluminium or steel. A printed part behaves completely differently, especially along layer lines.
  • The gate. The hinge, nose and gate-open strength are often the weakest part, and the hardest for a model to reason about.
Replace or confirm these with what actually happened: where did it fail? Did it contradict itself, hallucinate numbers, or produce something unprintable?

Takeaways
