A golf swing trainer from a Kinect and a Quest

Full-body tracking from a second-hand Xbox Kinect, streamed into a Meta Quest so I can see my own swing in VR.

I wanted to see my golf swing from the outside while I'm making it. Launch monitors and swing-analysis setups cost thousands. I had a Meta Quest 3, and second-hand Xbox Kinects are cheap, so I'm building my own swing trainer: the Kinect tracks my whole body and streams it into a Unity scene running in the Quest, so I can watch and correct my movement in VR.

Where it started

The first version of the idea needed no code at all. I mounted my phone on a tripod, streamed its camera over Wi-Fi, and opened the stream as a floating browser window in the Quest's passthrough mode. With the Quest's multitasking setting turned on, the window can sit beside a game like Golf+, so in theory you can play while watching yourself from the side. It worked as a novelty, but the 0.5 to 2 second delay made it useless for looking at a swing as it happens. That's what pushed me to proper body tracking.

The hardware

I bought a pre-owned Kinect for Xbox One (the newer, chunkier Kinect v2). It uses a proprietary plug, so on a PC it needs a "Kinect adapter for Windows / Xbox One S": a small hub with a power brick on one side and a USB 3.0 cable on the other. Microsoft stopped making the official one years ago, but generic ones are easy to find for around AU$30 to 45 (choose the AU plug).

Two things worth knowing if you try this:

  • It must be a real USB 3.0 port. Kinect is fussy about USB controllers. Use a port on the back of the PC, not a front-panel port or a hub.
  • Install the Kinect for Windows SDK 2.0 before plugging it in, then run the Kinect Configuration Verifier that comes with it to confirm everything works.

The software

The trainer is a Unity project written in C#. The Kinect side reads the body skeleton (joint positions for the whole body, many times a second) on the PC, and the Quest side renders that skeleton in the headset so I can see my swing in 3D.

Confirm or correct: how the Kinect data gets to the Quest (Quest Link or Air Link to a PC-run Unity app, or streamed over the network to a standalone build?), and whether you see an avatar, a stick figure or a mirror of yourself.

The hard part

The tricky problem is integration: getting two devices with different coordinate systems and different latencies to agree on where my body is. The Kinect measures everything relative to itself, sitting on a shelf in front of me; the Quest measures everything relative to the headset on my head. Until the two are lined up, my VR body floats in the wrong place, and the moment I turn my head the mismatch shows.

Describe how you're solving the alignment (a calibration pose, matching the head joint to the headset, a fixed offset) and how bad the lag is. A short clip or GIF of a swing would be the best thing on this page.

What's next

Where it's heading: club and ball tracking, comparing your swing against a reference swing, recording and replaying swings, and so on.
